\textbf{Remarks:}\par\vspace{4pt}
According to the Academic Instruction of the University, in the academic system, each Theoretical credit is presented in 16 hours, each Practical credit in 32 hours, and each Workshop credit in 48 hours.\par\vspace{8pt}
There are some different types of courses passed by a bachelor/master/Ph.D. student at this university, as listed below:
\begin{enumerate}
  \setlength{\itemsep}{1pt}
  \setlength{\parskip}{0pt}
  \item Basic
  \item Major
  \item Specialized
  \item Elective
  \item General
  \item Workshop
  \item Internship
  \item Project
\end{enumerate}
\vspace{4pt}
Each of these types contains some courses to be passed according to the educational regulations passed by the Ministry of Science, Research and Technology or the Ministry of Health \& Medical Education of Iran.\par\vspace{8pt}
The grading system in this university is from 0 to 20. The minimum passing grade for a course leading to an Associate's Degree or a Bachelor's Degree is 10, for a course leading to a Master's Degree and Medical Sciences is 12, and for a course leading to a Ph.D. Degree is 14.

\textbf{Title of the Course:} Introduction to Computing Systems and Programming\\
\textbf{Number of Credits:} 4\\
\textbf{Type of the Course:} Specialized | Theoretical\\
\textbf{Training hours:} 64h Theoretical\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Course Objectives:}\par\vspace{2pt}
Explanation of main computer parts, the concept of software and hardware, algorithms design, and
an introduction to a structured computer programming language.\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Syllabus of Courses:}\par\vspace{2pt}
Computer fundamentals, including the role of computers in the modern world and their\par
applications. Introduction to main computer parts and the concept of software and hardware.\par
Native data types (Integrals and Floating points). Algorithm design and problem-solving principles\par
(Flow, selection, and loops). Implanting the algorithm by Pseudo Code. An introduction to a\par
structured programming language. Constants, variables, computational and logical expressions.\par
language-specific instructions. Conditional structures. Loops, Arrays, vectors and matrices,\par
functions, and procedures. I/O and files operations. Implementing simple algorithms by the\par
programming language. Searching and sorting. Advanced program design.\par
Programming language: C/C++\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{TextBooks:}\par\vspace{2pt}
1. Bjarne Stroustrup, "The C++ Programming Language", Addison Wesley, 2004.\par
2. Stanley B Lippman, "The C++ Primer", Addison Wesley, 2005.\par
3. Paul Deitel \& Harvey Deitel, “C++ How to Program (Early Objects Version)”, Prentice Hall; 9\par
edition, 2013.\par

\textbf{Title of the Course:} Physics 1\\
\textbf{Number of Credits:} 3\\
\textbf{Type of the Course:} Basic | Theoretical\\
\textbf{Training hours:} 48h Theoretical\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Course Objectives:}\par\vspace{2pt}
The main goal of fundamental courses in physics is to provide tools by which students can learn
how to read scientific material effectively, identify fundamental concepts, reason through scientific
questions, and solve quantitative problems. Physics 1 is the first course of this set. This course
covers the fundamental concepts in Classical Mechanics and Thermodynamics.\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Syllabus of Courses:}\par\vspace{2pt}
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Units, Physical Quantities, and Vectors:}\par
The Nature of Physics, Solving Physics Problems, Standards and Units, Unit Consistency and\par
Conversions, Uncertainty and Significant Figures, Estimates and Orders of Magnitude, Vectors\par
and Vector Addition, Components of Vectors, Unit Vectors, Products of Vectors\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Motion along a Straight Line:}\par
Displacement, Time, and Average Velocity, Instantaneous Velocity, Average and Instantaneous\par
Acceleration, Motion with Constant Acceleration, Freely Falling Bodies, Velocity and Position by\par
Integration\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Motion in Two or Three Dimensions:}\par
Position and Velocity Vectors, The Acceleration Vector, Projectile Motion, Motion in a Circle,\par
Relative Velocity\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Newton’s Laws of Motion:}\par
Force and Interactions, Newton’s First Law, Newton’s Second Law, Mass and Weight, Newton’s\par
Third Law, Free-Body Diagrams\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Applying Newton’s Laws:}\par
Using Newton’s First Law: Particles in Equilibrium, Using Newton’s Second Law: Dynamics of\par
Particles, Frictional Forces, Dynamics of Circular Motion, The Fundamental Forces of Nature\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Work and Kinetic Energy:}\par
Work, Kinetic Energy, and the Work-Energy Theorem, Work and Energy with Varying Forces,\par
Power\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Potential Energy and Energy Conservation:}\par
Gravitational Potential Energy, Elastic Potential Energy, Conservative and Non-conservative\par
Forces, Force and Potential Energy, Energy Diagrams\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Momentum, Impulse, and Collisions:}\par
Momentum and Impulse, Conservation of Momentum, Momentum Conservation and Collisions,\par
Elastic Collisions, Center of Mass, Rocket Propulsion\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Rotation of Rigid Bodies:}\par
Angular Velocity and Acceleration, Rotation with Constant Angular Acceleration, Relating Linear\par
and Angular Kinematics, Energy in Rotational Motion, Parallel-Axis Theorem, Moment-of-Inertia\par
Calculations\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Dynamics of Rotational Motion:}\par
Torque, Torque and Angular Acceleration for a Rigid-Body, Rigid-Body Rotation About a Moving\par
Axis, Work and Power in Rotational Motion, Angular Momentum, Conservation of Angular\par
Momentum, Gyroscopes, and Precession\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Equilibrium and Elasticity:}\par
Conditions for Equilibrium, Center of Gravity, Solving Rigid-Body Equilibrium Problems, Stress,\par
Strain, Elastic Moduli, Elasticity, and Plasticity\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Fluid Mechanics:}\par
Density, Pressure in a Fluid, Buoyancy, Fluid Flow, Bernoulli’s Equation, Viscosity, and\par
Turbulence\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Gravitation:}\par
Newton’s Law of Gravitation, Weight, Gravitational Potential Energy, The Motion of Satellites,\par
Kepler’s Laws and the Motion of Planets, Spherical Mass Distributions, Apparent Weight and the\par
Earth’s Rotation, Black Holes\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Periodic Motion:}\par
Describing Oscillation, Simple Harmonic Motion, Energy in Simple Harmonic Motion,\par
Applications of Simple Harmonic Motion, The Simple Pendulum, The Physical Pendulum,\par
Damped Oscillations, Forced Oscillations, and Resonance\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Temperature and Heat:}\par
Temperature and Thermal Equilibrium, Thermometers and Temperature Scales, Gas\par
Thermometers and the Kelvin Scale, Thermal Expansion, Quantity of Heat, Calorimetry and Phase\par
Changes, Mechanisms of Heat Transfer\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Thermal Properties of Matter:}\par
Equations of State, Molecular Properties of Matter, Kinetic-Molecular Model of an Ideal Gas,\par
Heat Capacities, Molecular Speeds, Phases of Matter\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- The First Law of Thermodynamics:}\par
Thermodynamic Systems, Work Done During Volume Changes, Paths Between Thermodynamic\par
States, Internal Energy and the First Law of Thermodynamics, Kinds of Thermodynamic\par
Processes, Internal Energy of an Ideal Gas, Heat Capacities of an Ideal Gas, Adiabatic Processes\par
for an Ideal Gas\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- The Second Law of Thermodynamics:}\par
Directions of Thermodynamic Processes, Heat Engines, Internal-Combustion Engines,\par
Refrigerators, The Second Law of Thermodynamics, The Carnot Cycle, Entropy, Microscopic\par
Interpretation of Entropy\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{TextBooks:}\par\vspace{2pt}
1. Fundamentals of Physics, David Halliday, Jearl Walker, Robert Resnick, 10th edition\par

\textbf{Title of the Course:} Advanced Programming\\
\textbf{Number of Credits:} 3\\
\textbf{Type of the Course:} Specialized | Theoretical\\
\textbf{Pre-requisite:} Introduction to Computing Systems and Programming\\
\textbf{Training hours:} 48h Theoretical\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Course Objectives:}\par\vspace{2pt}
The purpose of this course is to study the principles and methods (Polymorphism, Inheritance,
Program Samples, and other paradigms of programming) necessary to produce a computer
program with good-quality specifications. In this regard, after covering the top-down design
method to solve the problem, students will be introduced to the concepts and techniques of objectoriented programming as a tool for managing complexity in medium and large-scale applications.
Program performance, testing, and debugging are focused on throughout the course. An objectoriented programming language (such as C++) is used to teach these principles and methods, and
students are introduced to the concepts and techniques of object-oriented programming and
module-based object design, advanced concepts such as graphical user interface design.\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Syllabus of Courses:}\par\vspace{2pt}
Introduction: Program life cycle, the importance of programming, programming paradigms,\par
Objected orientation\par
Object-oriented programming: Description of Objects, Object recognition, Finding implantable\par
objects in problems, Objects connection, Object-oriented programming language, an Introduction\par
to C++ and its history\par
A review of a functional programming language (C++), Polymorphism, inheritance, program\par
samples\par
Class and Objects, encapsulation and abstract, C++ classes and their parts (private, public, and\par
protected), constructor and destructors, derived classes\par
Polymorphism, polymorphism importance, function polymorphism, operator polymorphism\par
Inheritance: introduction to inheritance, single and multiple inheritances\par
Templates: template importance, function templates, derived data type templates\par
Other programming paradigms: introduction to event-oriented and graphical programming,\par
windows programming, client-side programming\par
Program testing and documentation: the importance of testing and documentation, automatic\par
testing tools, in-line documentation, technical documentation, user manuals\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{TextBooks:}\par\vspace{2pt}
1. Bjarne Stroustrup, "The C++ Programming Language", Addison Wesley, 2004.\par
2. Stanley B Lippman, "The C++ Primer", Addison Wesley, 2005.\par
3. Paul Deitel \& Harvey Deitel, “C++ How to Program (Early Objects Version)”, Prentice Hall; 9\par
edition, 2013.\par

\textbf{Title of the Course:} Differential Equations\\
\textbf{Number of Credits:} 3\\
\textbf{Type of the Course:} Basic | Theoretical\\
\textbf{Pre-requisite:} Calculus 1\\
\textbf{Training hours:} 48h Theoretical\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Course Objectives:}\par\vspace{2pt}
In this course, the first and second levels of linear differential equations and some nonlinear
differential equations will be introduced, in addition, students will learn about some numerical and
analytical ways to solve Mathematical Problems.\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{Syllabus of Courses:}\par\vspace{2pt}
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Introduction}\par
Some Basic Mathematical Models, Direction Fields, Solutions of Some Differential Equations,\par
Classification of Differential Equations, Historical Remarks\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- First Order Differential Equations}\par
Linear Equations with Variable Coefficients, Separable Equations, Modeling with First Order\par
Equations, Differences between Linear and Nonlinear Equations, Autonomous Equations and\par
Population Dynamics, Exact Equations and Integrating Factors, The Existence and Uniqueness\par
Theorem, First Order Difference Equations\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Second Order Linear Equations}\par
Homogeneous Equations with Constant Coefficients, Fundamental Solutions of Linear\par
Homogeneous Equations, Linear Independence and the Wronskian, Complex Roots of the\par
Characteristic Equation, Repeated Roots, Reduction of Order, Nonhomogeneous Equations,\par
Method of Undetermined Coefficients, Variation of Parameters.\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Higher Order Linear Equations}\par
General Theory of nth Order Linear Equations, Homogeneous Equations with Constant\par
Coefficients, The Method of Undetermined Coefficients, The Method of Variation of Parameters\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Series Solutions of Second-Order Linear Equations}\par
Review of Power Series, Series Solutions near an Ordinary Point, Regular Singular Points, Euler\par
Equations, Series Solutions near a Regular Singular Point, Bessel’s Equation\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- The Laplace Transform}\par
Definition of the Laplace Transform, Solution of Initial Value Problems, Step Functions,\par
Differential Equations with Discontinuous Forcing Functions, Impulse Functions, and The\par
Convolution Integral\par
\vspace{2pt}\textbf{- Systems of First-Order Linear Equations}\par
Introduction, Review of Matrices, Systems of Linear Algebraic Equations; Linear Independence,\par
Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors, Basic Theory of Systems of First Order Linear Equations\par
Homogeneous Linear Systems with Constant Coefficients, Complex Eigenvalues, Fundamental\par
Matrices, Repeated Eigenvalues,\par\vspace{4pt}
\textbf{TextBooks:}\par\vspace{2pt}
1. E. Kreyszig, Advanced Engineering Mathematics, 1999\par
2. P. Blanchard, R.L. Devaney, and Co. Hall, Differential Equations, 1st Edition, Brooks/ColePub,\par
1998\par
